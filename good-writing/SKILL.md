# Good Writing

Use revision to discover what the author means and what is worth saying. Aim for a worthwhile idea developed to an appropriate depth, in prose that lets the reader follow it naturally.

This skill adapts Paul Graham's [Good Writing](https://www.paulgraham.com/goodwriting.html), May 2025. The workflow below is an implementation of its ideas, not a fixed procedure prescribed by Graham.

## Establish the editing scope

Follow the requested deliverable, language, audience, voice, and length. Infer reasonable defaults from the supplied text. If the user wants proofreading only, make only the requested surface corrections; do not expand the assignment into a substantive rewrite.

For development or deep revision, begin with a working understanding of the question, the intended point, and why it matters. Let that understanding change as the writing reveals a clearer thought. Preserve the author's intent; make consequential changes to the thesis or meaning explicit rather than silently substituting your own position.

## Work between thought and expression

Treat an awkward sentence, an irritating repetition, or a paragraph that will not fit as a place to investigate. Ask what it actually needs to say and why the reader needs it. The problem may be wording, but it may also be a duplicated thought, an unstated relationship, a misplaced qualification, or a conclusion that is not worth pursuing.

Try a small, relevant change: shorten or expand a passage, state a relationship directly, split or join sentences, or move a point to where the reader can understand it. Compare the revision with the original for meaning, usefulness, and ease of reading. Reject a smoother candidate if it distorts a claim, drops a necessary qualification, or adds unsupported content. Do not impose compression, a quota of alternatives, or elaborate formal constraints.

Read the result as a reader encountering the argument for the first time. Can the reader follow how one sentence leads to the next? Does each idea receive the space it needs? Does a late addition interrupt the main path? If a branch cannot fit naturally, reconsider its position or whether it belongs in a note.

Attend to rhythm while checking meaning. A short sentence can state a simple point; a longer one may be needed to carry a qualification or a subtle relationship. Let the structure follow the thought. Neither uniform brevity nor decorative variation is a goal.

Allow discoveries from any of these changes to alter the wording, organization, or argument again. Thought and expression need not be edited in separate phases. Revisit passages where a real problem remains, and stop when further changes offer little improvement beyond taste. There is no required rereading count.

## Keep the source's limits visible

Use this loop where writing helps develop an idea. When a passage reports an experiment, something built, or someone else's ideas, its prose does not establish the quality of the underlying work. The distinction concerns how the ideas were developed, not a blanket ban on a genre: a report can contain both factual reporting and developing interpretation.

Use ease of reading as a prompt to investigate meaning, never as proof of correctness. Awkward prose does not by itself justify rejecting a claim. Internal consistency cannot establish that its premises match the world, and an honest author can still be mistaken.

Keep supplied facts, results, and uncertainty intact. If a revision depends on a missing fact, retain the uncertainty or briefly identify the gap; do not manufacture support. Resolve only what the available material warrants. This is a boundary on revision, not a mandatory research-audit workflow.

## Deliver the requested writing

For a draft or substantive revision, return complete, usable text unless the user requested an outline or critique. Add a brief explanation only for consequential conceptual changes or material unresolved questions. For proofreading, return the requested clean text or differences.

Before returning it, check that the main point is worthwhile and clear, the reader can follow the relationships, and no improvement in flow has changed the facts or concealed uncertainty. Do not require a displayed argument map, checklist, or account of the editing process.
